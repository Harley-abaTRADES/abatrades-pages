Phase 2 · Proposed content

Fourteen seconds is how long we have to make a sparky stop scrolling

Six proposed angles for aba ELECTRICAL recruitment, with the exact copy written and the reasoning attached. Every one is traced back to something the phase 1 research actually found. The words are finished. The commercial terms are not mine to decide, and they are flagged in red throughout.

01Method

How these were derived

Not brainstormed. Each concept starts from a pattern measured across 27 live competitor ads, and the copy is written to the shape that pattern implies.

58%of long-running ads lead with a take-home vehicle
42%state a pay figure outright
33%use the frustration hook, against 19% of the library
1apprentice ad found in the entire market

Two things shape everything below. First, the plain hiring announcement is the default and it is not what wins, so none of these six open with one. Second, the offers are not mine to write. The data says state the pay, and I have marked where the pay goes rather than inventing a number.

02Evidence

What we are building from

The four findings that drive the angles. Full working is in the phase 1 research page.

The hook that over-performs is the one aimed at their current boss

Naming the candidate's frustration is 33% of the ads that lasted 60 days or more, against 19% of everything published. Nothing else over-performs its share by that much.

The take-home vehicle is the market's top currency and nobody has claimed it visually

It shows up in 58% of the survivors. Bice names it in copy and still uses a group photo, so the hero shot of a kitted ute is sitting there unclaimed.

Time outsells money as a differentiator

Half the long-running ads sell the schedule: four day weeks, TOIL, RDOs, early finishes. Worth testing against the rate rather than assuming one beats the other.

Two things here no competitor can copy

The 25 metre in-house training wall, and suburb level honesty about where the work is. Our sweep found no Adelaide advertiser stating its actual service area beyond the word Adelaide.

04Plan

How we would run them

Two campaigns, not six experiments. The audiences are different and running them together would just confuse the signal.

Campaign 1 · Qualified electricians

Concepts A, B, C, D and F live together in one ad set as always-on. Let the platform allocate. Five creatives is enough to learn which angle carries without starving any of them.

Campaign 2 · Apprentices

Concept E on its own, always-on, separate budget. Different audience, different message, and the market has left it almost entirely uncontested so there is little to react to.

Format

Every concept gets a static and a 9:16 vertical at 15 to 30 seconds. Start with statics for A and F, video for B, C, D and E since those are the ones where motion earns its place.

The discipline bit

Judge on applications, not on how good the creative looks. The Ad Library can tell us what competitors are running but nothing about how it performed, so our own numbers have to come from the ad account.

What I would run first

Concept A and Concept D. A is the highest conviction angle in the data, and D is the only claim in this market nobody can answer. B goes up alongside them because the visual is unclaimed and it costs very little to test.

05Decisions

What we need from you

Eight answers. Every one of them changes the copy, so the sooner they land the sooner these become real assets rather than a proposal.

The destination URL, today

The new page is join-or-team-v2 and still in draft. The live one is join-our-team. Every ad needs one, and they are different campaigns depending on which you pick.

The pay band

The single biggest lever in the data. Pay is stated in 42% of the ads that last, and the Q Home Services ad leads with $65 an hour. This is Paul’s call, and the whole offer list depends on it. Say the number, say above award, or say nothing. Say nothing is the weakest of the three.

The vehicle policy

Do we actually provide a take-home ute or van? Concept B puts it on the front of the ad, so if the answer is no, that concept changes shape.

Time perks

RDO, TOIL, early Friday finishes, a four day week. Which of these are real and which do we want to commit to in writing? Concept C leads with it.

The sign-on bonus

A sign-on bonus appears in 42% of the survivors. If we do not offer one we can still compete on the other lines, but it is worth knowing.

The apprentice intake

Are we taking on apprentices, how many, and for which year levels? Concept E cannot run without it.

Showing the training wall

Can we photograph and film the 25 metre wall, and are we willing to invite people to see it? Concept D rests on it.

Meta ad account access

We cannot measure our own results without it, and the Ad Library gives us nothing on our own ads. This is the difference between running ads and learning from them.
