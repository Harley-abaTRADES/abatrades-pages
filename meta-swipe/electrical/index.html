Meta recruitment creative: what is actually working for electricians in Australia

A swipe file of 11 live recruitment ads from the Meta Ad Library, ranked by how long they have been running, with the patterns pulled out. Prepared for review before we move to proposed content and then creative.

How this was built

Every ad here is live right now and was pulled from the public Meta Ad Library on 28 October 2026, filtered to Australia. Nothing is copied from a third party list. The ranking signal is longevity, because Meta only shows active ads for ordinary commercial advertisers, so an ad still running is an ad that is earning its place.

The one caveat: the Ad Library shows active ads only, so this cannot tell us what historically worked and then stopped. It shows what is working now. Video ads are under-represented in this sweep because the library renders them as thumbnails; a separate video pass is a sensible phase 1b.

The patterns that repeat

These are the things the long-running ads do in common. Not one of them is a clever visual trick.

1. Pay is stated outright

Five of the eleven name a number. $38 to $45 an hour, $110k to $135k a year, up to $65 an hour in Queensland. The ones that hide the rate are the ones that do not last.

2. Perks are itemised, never summarised

The long-running ads list eight to ten single-line perks with ticks. Nobody writes generous package. They write the take-home ute, the fuel card, the birthday off, the Thursday finish.

3. The take-home vehicle is the top currency

It is the perk that gets named first and described in most detail. Bice leads with the van and the tool kit; Activ8 leads with personal use on the fuel card.

4. Time beats money as a differentiator

Five of eleven sell the schedule: four-day weeks, TOIL banking, RDOs, early Friday finishes, Monday to Thursday. At this pay level time is the thing competitors cannot easily match.

5. The hook is your current employer, not us

The strongest copy names the reader's private frustration. Does your boss actually value your work. Ready to enjoy being a sparky again. It reads like a person, not a job spec.

6. Location is named by suburb, not by state

Unley and the coastline south of the Adelaide CBD. Moreton Bay. Mount Gambier. Coffs Harbour. Specific geography signals a real local job to someone scrolling in that area.

7. Non-negotiables are framed as integrity

We do follow the rules. Certificate III, sorry, non-negotiable. Stating the hard requirements plainly reads as a company that will not cut corners, which tradespeople screen for.

8. The creative is mostly low production

Text on a photo, or a plain graphic card, dominates. Only a handful use real branded people and vehicles, and almost nobody in this sweep is running video.

Where aba TRADES can win

These are the gaps in the set. Every one of them is something we already hold the raw material for.

Nobody makes the vehicle the hero image

Bice names the van in copy but the creative is a group photo holding signs. A clean shot of a kitted-out take-home ute with the perk line on it is an unclaimed visual.

Almost nobody shows real team photography well

Only two advertisers use real people, and neither puts genuine craft into it. Our SharePoint library holds 65 professional electrical on-site shots at up to 8192px.

Only one apprentice creative in the whole sweep

Sorted Electrical is the single apprentice ad found. Apprentice recruitment meta is close to uncontested, and we have the training wall to back it.

Training is treated as a perk, not a system

Everyone offers ongoing training. Nobody can point at a facility. Our 25 metre in-house training wall is a physical, photographable, uncopyable proof point.

Video is barely used

This sweep is static-only by design, but it is worth a separate video pass. If the market is this static-heavy, vertical video is a cheap edge.
