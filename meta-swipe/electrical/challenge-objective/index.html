The objective: the aba ELECTRICAL recruitment microgame

For: the agent taking the creative brief From: Harley (aba TRADES operations agent) Date: 28 October 2026 Status: the objective, the commercial constraints and the guardrails are FIXED. The creative treatment is ours to win.

If you read nothing else: make a page that a qualified electrician opens on his phone from an ad, has genuine fun with for about twenty seconds, walks away knowing exactly what aba ELECTRICAL offers without ever feeling sold to, and taps Apply. Then wants to send it to a mate. Difficulty is the enemy. This page must never lose a good candidate.

Supersedes the earlier brief This objective replaces the build brief at meta-swipe/electrical/challenge-brief/, which describes the three round concept. That concept has been rejected and the reasons are in section 7, so read this instead.

1. What we are trying to achieve

Two results, in order.

  1. Applications from qualified Adelaide electricians, cheaper than a straight offer ad. This is a recruitment asset, not a brand awareness exercise. It is judged on applications and cost per qualified applicant against the plain offer ads running beside it.
  2. Circulation inside the trade. If a sparky sends it to another sparky, we get reach we did not pay for, and a recommendation from a mate is worth more than any ad we can buy.

Everything else (clicks, time on page, completion rate) is instrumentation, not the goal. A game is a bigger ask than a form, so click through may well look worse on paper. That is expected and accepted. What is not acceptable is a game that costs us a candidate who would have applied.

2. Who is on the other end

A qualified electrician, roughly mid twenties to forties, on a phone, quite possibly in the ute or on site with one thumb and gloves in his pocket. He gives a page about ten seconds to earn more.

  • He knows his trade cold and he is proud of it.
  • He is allergic to being marketed at. He has seen a thousand job ads promising the world with no number attached.
  • He spots a lie instantly, and a wrong technical detail loses him faster than a bad joke does.
  • He enjoys a laugh at the expense of the other trades.
  • He does not want a hobby. He has a page in front of him for one moment and then he is gone.

3. The human insight

He is the person everyone calls when something is wrong. Being the one who knows is the whole appeal of the trade, and being recognised for knowing is the strongest thing we can give him in twenty seconds.

So the page should treat him as an expert, and give him a moment that only a person who understands this job would enjoy. That is the difference between flattery and a test: a test says prove it, this page says you already know.

4. The required shape

  1. The ad is ours, not yours. It contains no rate and no offer.
  2. The page opens straight into the fun. No form, no email, no gate, no loading screen, no cookie wall, no sign up.
  3. The fun takes about twenty seconds and cannot be failed in a way that matters. A wrong move gets a laugh and costs nothing.
  4. The offer lands as a by product of playing, not as a block of sales copy. He should finish knowing the four things the job actually comes with.
  5. The exit is the application. When the fun is over, the primary action is Apply. Nothing on this page competes with that.
  6. The share is one tap, pre-written, and takes the mate straight into the same thing.

5. The offer facts, and the only facts allowed

The offer lives on the join our team page and is maintained there. Where the page quotes it, these four are the whole of it:

  • $60 an hour, flat, T&Cs apply. Most competitors write a range. A flat number is ours to own.
  • A company ute and a fuel card. Take-home, and the card runs it.
  • A 25 metre in-house training wall, and developing tradespeople since 2015.
  • Work close to home, across Adelaide.

Never invent a benefit, and never round a fact up. Specifically do not include: a sign on bonus (there is none), apprenticeships (none this intake), a four day week, an RDO or TOIL (those belong to the plumbing division, not electrical). Never write the phrase "personal use" anywhere near the ute; a competitor advertises that and we cannot match it, so the copy stays "take-home, and the card runs it".

Naming what we do NOT offer is fair game and has worked well: an honest "there is no sign on bonus, we would rather say so" reads as straight dealing in a category full of waffle.

6. Fixed constraints

Tone: tongue in cheek, dry, Australian, confident. Never corporate, never try hard, never a smirk at the candidate's expense. The joke is always either on the situation or on the other trades, never on the applicant.

Brand: navy #1D3B6D, secondary #2E528E, slate #7789A7, dark #0B1D3B, footer #172F57, surfaces #EBEDF2 / #F5F6F8, border #D3D4DA, electrical accent #DC7E49 used as the single accent. Futura PT via https://use.typekit.net/aym5yls.css, verified as actually loading rather than silently falling back. Logo https://harley-abatrades.github.io/abatrades-pages/assets/brand/abatrades-logo-h.svg. Trademark is lowercase "aba", the division is "aba ELECTRICAL", the business name is always "aba TRADES". Keep solar silent in all copy.

People: real staff only. No AI generated humans, ever. The pitch is a real team and a real 25 metre training wall, so a synthetic sparky undermines the product.

Technical: one static HTML page, inline CSS, plain JavaScript. No framework, no game engine, no third party script, no build step, no backend, no account. It has to be readable and maintainable by us afterwards.

  • Mobile first. Test at 390px, then 320px. One thumb, no horizontal scroll.
  • Every interactive target at least 44px, measured at BOTH widths, not eyeballed.
  • No em dashes in any copy. Standing house rule, they read as AI written.
  • No orphan word on a heading's last line.
  • Playable with prefers-reduced-motion, and keyboard reachable where it reasonably can be.
  • Renders correctly with JavaScript off as far as possible: a readable page with the offer and a working Apply link.
  • No data collection before any form. No tracking beyond what Meta already has.
  • Loads on 4G in under a second to interactive.

7. What has already been tried, and why it was rejected

This is the most valuable part of this brief. Every one of these was built or specified, then killed for a reason. Do not rebuild them.

Shape Why it failed
Three rounds, timed final round A gauntlet. Slow readers and first timers are punished, and the whole point is to lose nobody.
One round, "spot the cooked breaker", scored on TIME Time punishes reading the instructions first, and a refresh resets it, so the score is unfair AND meaningless.
A daily puzzle, Wordle shaped, attempts as the score, spoiler free share grid The research behind it is real (one puzzle a day, same for everyone, spoiler free grid, sharing in group chats) but a recruit opens this ONCE from an ad. There is no day two. Do not make it a habit product.
Drag eight tools into six cutouts, scored on wrong tools Better, but it tested the player's own kit knowledge. The page's job is to promote what WE offer, not to examine him.

The rule that came out of all four: never score this page on time, and never make it a test. Score nothing, or score mistakes, and keep the difficulty so low that the only people who bail are people who were never going to apply.

8. The current best attempt, and what is wrong with it

Live at https://harley-abatrades.github.io/abatrades-pages/challenge/electrical/ (a review build, behind a red "not live" flag).

It is a drag and drop: four labelled spots in a branded ute box ($60 an hour, company ute and fuel card, 25 metre training wall, work close to home) and six cards on the bench, where the two ring ins are the things we do NOT offer (a sign on bonus, a four day week) and each gets an honest one liner. Pack all four and the offer is revealed in full, then the primary button is Apply.

What works: the mechanic carries the offer, the ring ins are funny and honest, nothing is timed, and it cannot be failed.

What is weak, and where you should beat it: it is a sorting exercise wearing a costume, it looks like every other clean navy site, the fun is polite rather than actually funny, and there is nothing in it a sparky would send to a mate because he wanted to, rather than because there is a share button.

9. What "better" means

Not more features, and not more polish. Specifically:

  • Funnier. A joke a sparky would repeat to another sparky in the ute.
  • Faster to understand. He should know what to do before he has finished reading the sentence telling him what to do.
  • More distinctive. Recognisably ours, not a template with our navy applied.
  • Worth sending. The share should happen because the thing is good, not because we asked.
  • Still selling without selling. He finishes knowing the four facts, and those four facts are the only claims we make.

10. Acceptance criteria

  1. Loads and is playable at 390px with one thumb, no horizontal scroll, and at 320px without breaking.
  2. Every interactive target is at least 44px at both widths, measured.
  3. It can be completed, comfortably, by somebody who is not paying full attention.
  4. Nothing about it can be failed, or the failure costs nothing.
  5. The four offer facts land, and no other benefit is claimed anywhere.
  6. The primary action at the end is Apply, going to the join our team page (the URL is one constant).
  7. Share is one tap, pre-written, and does not spoil the fun for the person receiving it.
  8. No em dashes, no orphan heading words, no third party script, one HTML file plus assets.
  9. With JavaScript off the page still reads and the Apply link still works.
  10. Nothing complains on a 4G phone: no layout shift, no waiting.
