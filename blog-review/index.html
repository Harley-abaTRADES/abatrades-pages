the gap

The plan and reality pull in different directions

The flagship "click-bait" titles in the FY25-26 plan mostly do not match what people actually search. Of the plan titles we confirmed live, over half have earned zero organic clicks in the window.

What actually ranks

How-to + cost queries

"error codes", "how much does it cost", "how to fix", "best temperature". Users search problems, not seasons.

What the plan preferred

Seasonal click-bait

"Toilet Replacement vs Repair", "Spring Gas Safety Checklist", "The Summer Decision That Affects Your Water Bills". Good titles, low search demand.

verdict

Is the agency doing a good job?

Good where the reward lives, weak at the plan itself.

They have a clear, working SEO playbook: the troubleshooting and how-to articles on Air Con and Plumbing rank and pull genuine demand with strong read-engagement. That is real capability.

But four of six brands have no blog presence at all, and the content plan they wrote is not producing traffic where it has been published. The calendar reads well and does not match search reality.

for the meeting

Five questions to put to the agency

1

Why no blog on 4 brands?

Electrical, Roofing, Renovations and the group site have essentially no article presence. Was coverage intentional?

2

Plan vs search demand

Why do seasonal click-bait titles keep being planned when the data shows problem-based how-to queries are what ranks and pulls?

3

Traffic to conversion

0.9% and 0.7% of leads land on blog articles. How are articles meant to capture leads, or are they purely a ranking asset?

4

What are their KPIs

Are they accountable to rankings and clicks, or to leads and revenue? The answer changes how you read their reports.

5

Replicate the winner

The Air Con formula is proven. What is the plan to run the same method across Plumbing's full set and into the other four brands?

6

Attribution wiring

Offer to sink lead events into the dashboard so blog value is tracked in leads, not just clicks, next quarter.

Bottom line

They are not uniformly good or bad. They are strong where it pays and missing on most of the map. Walk in holding the click and lead numbers, and ask for the plan to match them.

Method

Prepared by Harley, aba TRADES support team, for the agency meeting.

  • Window: 1 Jul 2025 to 29 Sep 2026 (the FY25-26 plan period plus current month).
  • Organic clicks, impressions and average search position: Google Search Console, per brand site, page-level.
  • Article pages identified by URL pattern and cross-checked against the plan's published titles; blog contribution = article-page clicks as a share of each brand's total organic clicks.
  • Reader engagement: GA4 sessions, engaged sessions and average session length for the article page set.
  • Lead attribution: GA4 lead events (form, click-to-call, book-now, chat, product enquiry) attributed to the landing page of the converting session. This is a landing-page cut, so it understates blog value for users who read an article and convert on a later session or page.
  • Lead channel: GA4 session default channel group of the converting session; "Cross-network" (Google Ads automated) excluded from the Organic/Paid columns.
  • GA4 path-exploration and supervised assisted-conversion reporting are not exposed through the automated data API, so a true multi-touch path is not included here.
