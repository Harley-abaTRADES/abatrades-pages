Chapter 1

Re-engagement reality

Over a 6-month window, 27.2% of members start a genuinely new job. Roughly 1 in every 4.

Non-members? 7.7%. Members come back more than three times as often.

What counts as a "new job" here: a fresh engagement is a genuinely new call-out, not a follow-on visit from a prior quote, and not a recall child of an earlier job. This removes the investigation-then-dispatch double-count that inflates raw job counts.

The definition that matters

A fresh engagement is a genuinely new call-out, not a follow-on from a prior quote and not a recall child of an earlier job. That strips out the investigation-to-dispatch double-count that inflates raw job numbers. So a member who books an aircon job a week after their plumbing job counts as a genuinely new job: both are fresh engagements, and that second one is the re-engagement we're chasing.

Dig in: ServiceTitan Reporting API → Operations → "Parent Job" report (id 453741926), field ParentJobNumber. One distinct parent job against a customer = one engagement.

Two numbers do different jobs here. The 27.2% is how many members start a fresh job in a half-year (incidence). The 592 in Chapter 2 is how many ever start a second one across 22 months (repeat). Same definition, two different questions.

Chapter 2

Across 22 months, 592 repeat

Of the 2,417 members who held a membership in the window, 592 (24.5%) came back for a second fresh engagement, a repeat, two genuinely new jobs from the same member.

The median gap between a member's genuine new jobs is ~53 days. Members re-engage, on their own, roughly every eight weeks.

Why this stack, and what we said no to

Three deliberate choices, each measured against the obvious alternative.

Why not ServiceTitan Marketing Pro?

The obvious in-platform option, but its email template and campaign building is UI-only. The API exposes campaign metadata and attribution, not template, sequence or launch endpoints. We can't automate a journey we can only click-build, and every change needs a human in the UI.

Our path: keep ServiceTitan as the source of truth (members, service type, consent) and run the journey on a real ESP. Revenue attribution stays in ServiceTitan via campaign tagging.

Why Brevo over ActiveCampaign / MailerLite?

Native triggered-journey builder, plain-text support (no forced HTML templates), and built-in unsubscribe/suppression compliance, the things a legal marketing send actually needs. Free tier covers our whole pilot (300 emails/day vs ~1,283 members).

The others: ActiveCampaign's cost scales fast at our volume ($79+/mo territory); MailerLite is cheaper but lighter on the branching depth this flow needs. Brevo leaves room to grow into SMS and variant testing later.

Why Harley crons over n8n?

Brevo's journey builder already owns the state machine, member position, day offsets, branching, suppression. The glue layer is only two thin, deterministic jobs: new members in → Brevo, and unsubscribes out → ServiceTitan. Two small scheduled scripts do that for $0; n8n's API requires a paid plan (~US$20-24/mo) to even build against.

Same compliance bar: consent writes are fixed field-mapping, never AI-judged, identical determinism either way. n8n earns its place later if orchestration grows (SMS write-backs, seasonal campaigns, multi-system syncs); until then it's a cost without a job.

Consent and marketing-status changes stay logic-based and foolproof, deterministic automation, never AI judgement, on anything customer-facing.

What we'll learn

This MVP isn't a revenue commitment. It's validated learning on the smallest possible test.

If it moves

Keep it running and layer in the next phases, richer creative, SMS, seasonal campaigns. The sequence is live from day one; a positive read confirms it stays.

If it doesn't

We learn cheap. Adjust the cadence, the copy, or the channel mix, and if nothing moves the number, we stop before investing in the next phases. The infrastructure is reusable either way.

Future phases, once the channel is proven

  • Richer creative: layer in branded HTML templates, the aba TRADES look, per-trade accent colours, imagery from the blog posts we link to. Plain text earns trust first; the branded layer comes once we know the channel performs.
  • SMS: same journey logic over text, with Podium's opt-out list respected (ServiceTitan write-back already designed).
  • Seasonal campaigns: database-wide blasts stay separate from this evergreen flow, the flow onboards, the campaigns activate.
  • Variant testing: the Plain/Spark drafts in this deck become real A/B tests in Brevo, let the data pick the voice.

Either way: a clean baseline, a directional read, and the plumbing in place for whatever comes next.
